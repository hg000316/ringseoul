Why it's harder than it looks

Korea's best places don't take online bookings.

The problem isn't that you don't speak Korean. It's that the shop's only booking channel is a phone call — answered by a person, in Korean, during business hours.

01

The Korean number wall

Korean apps and booking pages check for a domestic number and 본인인증 (ID verification). A +1, +44 or +61 number doesn't just fail quietly — the form won't accept you at all.

02

Phone only, Korean only

The one-chair salon, the twelve-seat counter, the neighbourhood clinic: much of what makes a trip memorable takes bookings by phone. No website, no English, no email.

03

The deposit you can't send

Some places hold a table only after a deposit by Korean bank transfer. No Korean card, no booking — so travellers give up, or ask a Korean friend to call on their behalf.

That's the gap we fill. You send the link. We do the talking.

Questions

The things people ask before their first call.

Can I book before I arrive in Korea?
Yes — that's the point. Because you don't need a Korean number, there's nothing to wait for: send your request from home, a week or two ahead for popular places, and the confirmation lands in your inbox before you fly. Landing today? Send it as early as you can and we'll call during the shop's opening hours.
Do I need a Korean phone number or Naver account?
No. No Korean number, no Naver or Kakao account, no 본인인증 (identity verification). We call from a Korean line and book under the name you give us. All we need from you is an email address.
What happens if the shop says no?
You pay nothing. We offer your second-choice time, and if that's gone too we email you what the shop said and suggest alternatives nearby. The $6 applies only to a booking that actually gets confirmed.
What language do I use?
Write to us in English. Our agent speaks natural Korean on the phone, handles the shop's questions, and sends your confirmation back in English — the time, the address, and what to say when you walk in.
Do I pay the restaurant through you?
No. You pay the shop directly, as you would anywhere. The $6 covers the call and the booking — not the meal, the cut or the treatment. We never take a cut of what you spend there.
How quickly will I hear back?
Most requests are called in the same day, within shop opening hours. Booking for tonight? Send it early — small places fill up, and many close between lunch and dinner.
Can you book anything in Korea?
No — and we say so up front. Restaurants, independent salons, nail and lash studios, clinics and 한의원 are in. Places that need a deposit by Korean bank transfer, shops that only take bookings through CatchTable or Naver, and ticketed events are out for now. If your request can't work, we tell you before you're charged.
